FREQUENTLY ASKED

Commercial litigation, answered.

What is commercial litigation in Singapore?

Commercial litigation refers to court proceedings arising from business disputes, including contract breaches, shareholder disputes, debt claims and commercial wrongdoing.

Which courts hear commercial disputes in Singapore?

Most commercial claims are heard in the State Courts: the Magistrate’s Court for claims up to S$60,000 and the District Court for claims up to S$250,000. Larger claims go to the General Division of the High Court. The Singapore International Commercial Court (SICC) hears international commercial disputes where the parties have agreed in writing to its jurisdiction, or where a case is transferred to it from the High Court.

What is the Singapore International Commercial Court (SICC)?

The SICC is a division of the Singapore High Court for international commercial disputes. Its bench includes international judges from common law and civil law systems, and registered foreign lawyers can appear in some cases (known as offshore cases). It offers flexible procedures suited to cross-border disputes.

What are the costs involved in commercial litigation?

Costs depend on the nature of the dispute, length of proceedings and expert involvement. Courts may award costs to the successful party, but a costs award rarely covers all of the legal fees actually incurred.

Can commercial disputes be resolved without going to court?

Yes. Many disputes are resolved through negotiation, mediation or arbitration, often saving time and cost.

What is summary judgment in Singapore?

Summary judgment allows courts to resolve disputes without trial where the defendant has no real defence to the claim. It is commonly used in clear contractual claims, such as unpaid debts.

How are contractual disputes resolved in Singapore?

Contractual disputes are resolved by interpreting contractual terms under the contract’s governing law and assessing breaches, losses and remedies.

What documents should businesses preserve before litigation?

Contracts, correspondence, financial records and internal communications, including emails and chat messages, should be preserved immediately.

How does evidence work in commercial litigation?

Evidence typically includes contracts, correspondence, financial records and witness testimony, subject to procedural rules.

Why is early legal advice important in disputes?

Early advice preserves rights, strengthens strategy and improves outcomes. Time limits also apply: most contract and tort claims must be brought within six years.

Can I get urgent relief before the case is decided?

Yes. Where there is a real risk that assets will be moved or evidence destroyed, the court can grant urgent orders, such as a freezing injunction or a search order, sometimes before the other side is notified.
